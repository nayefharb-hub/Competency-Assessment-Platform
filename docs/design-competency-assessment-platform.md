## Problem Statement

A web platform for assessing staff against competency frameworks. The long-term
vision is an open, multi-tenant product: any organization imports or defines a
framework (domain → competency → indicator, with a scoring mechanism at each
level) and assesses its people against it, sold later by public subscription.

The prototype is deliberately narrower. It serves KIB's PMO: assess project
managers against a single framework the founder has already built — an IPMA
ICB4 v4.0.1 framework (28 competence elements, 132 active Key Competence
Indicators, APM 0–5 Application scale, per-CE targets, and a Role-Ready /
Minor-Gap / Capability-Deficit rollup). The prototype must be fast to build and
usable by PMs to complete a real assessment this cycle.

## Demand Evidence

Two real drivers are stacked here; keep them distinct.

- **A funded internal mandate (verified).** The founder's manager — the Head of
  Strategy — requested the PM competency assessment, and it is a committed line in
  this year's business plan. That is a genuine sponsor above the founder and a
  real deadline. The founder himself is Head of PMO and sole assessor over a team
  of 9 (7 direct PMs, 2 dotted-line, 1 portfolio manager) — the internal champion
  and operator, not the person who commissioned it.
- **The mandate is for the outcome, not the web app.** "Assess PM competencies
  this year" is satisfiable by the existing Excel workbook. Demand for a *web
  platform* over the workbook is still not separately verified. The two are
  different products; only the first is on the business plan.
- **The Excel de-risks the mandate — use that.** Because the workbook already
  meets this year's deliverable, the web app is freed to be an honest **product
  test with KIB as design partner**, not a must-ship dependency of the business
  plan. Do not let the app's timeline put the mandate at risk; the spreadsheet is
  the fallback that already satisfies it.
- **Personal product ambition (second driver).** The founder wants to offer this
  by subscription and sell it later. Real motivation, layered on top of the
  internal mandate — but commercial demand is thesis-only: no non-KIB org has
  asked for or committed to it. It does not drive prototype scope.
- **Real validation questions (still open):** do the 7 PMs actually complete the
  assessment online, and does the output actually change the training-plan and
  project-assignment decisions the framework exists to inform?

## Status Quo

Users' current tool is the Excel workbook the founder built. It is genuinely
well-designed: versioned control IDs, target provenance, snapshot rules,
inactive-control handling, and a weighted per-competence-element rollup. A PM can
be fully assessed with it today.

**Scale reality-check (from the brief):** this is 9 people and one assessor
(the founder). "Collection from many PMs" is 9 files reviewed by one person, not
a 50-PM merge problem. So the *scale* pains below are modest at KIB today; the
pains that actually justify software here are the **behavioral** one (completion)
and the **structural** ones Excel cannot do cleanly (target-hiding + manager-blind
reconciliation, and cheap re-runs while the framework and scale are still moving).
Weight the four pains with that in mind — at KIB's size, they argue for the
*product test*, not for rescuing a scale emergency that doesn't exist yet.

The founder named four concrete pains, all of which the spreadsheet handles
badly at scale — and all of which are about *running and aggregating*
assessments, not about *defining* frameworks:

1. **Collection** — emailing the file to many PMs, chasing them, merging
   versions back. Painful at 10+ PMs, chaos at 50.
2. **No PMO-wide rollup** — each file is one person; there is no live view of
   where the whole PM population sits against targets.
3. **Self / manager / agreed flow** — the two-party reconciliation (PM
   self-scores, manager scores, agree in a 1-on-1) is clumsy to run and track in
   a shared file.
4. **Trends over cycles** — year-over-year comparison and gap-closing between
   cycles is hard to maintain in spreadsheets.

Plus a fifth, behavioral: the founder believes PMs are more likely to *finish*
an assessment online than by "sitting and filling out an Excel sheet at their
desk." Completion rate is a real wedge — but this specific claim is currently an
assumption, not something the founder has watched happen.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Three distinct roles, do not conflate them:**
- **Assessee:** a KIB project manager (7 direct) who completes the assessment.
- **Assessor / operator / champion:** the Head of PMO (the founder), sole
  assessor, who scores, runs the 1-on-1s, reads the rollup, and acts on it
  (training plans, project assignment).
- **Sponsor:** the Head of Strategy, who commissioned it as a business-plan item
  and to whom the program's completion is reported.

**Narrowest wedge (chosen):** a single-framework prototype — ICB4 only. Build
effort goes into the assessment loop: a PM self-scores online → manager scores →
they agree → the PMO sees a live rollup. This is the smallest version that proves
the two things software can do that the spreadsheet cannot: raise completion, and
give the PMO a live population view.

**Included: a single-framework admin editor.** Because the framework is still
being tuned, the prototype includes an admin/owner screen to edit *this one
framework* — but scoped to the **tunable layer**, which is what your Excel
already exposes as editable columns:
- set the required target level per indicator (and pick the active benchmark
  profile — Intermediate now)
- toggle each indicator active / inactive (with the written reason)
- set priority per indicator (high / medium / low)
- edit the **KIB guidance** column and the reason/rationale text

**Decided — ICB4 source text stays locked.** The editor never touches ICB4
indicator/description/measures text (preserves year-over-year comparison and the
"reproduced as published" posture, per §7). Instead, each indicator carries an
editable **KIB additional-info / clarification field** alongside the source text —
KIB context and plain-language clarifications, clearly marked as KIB's own, never
replacing the framework text. This is the only free-text authoring in the
prototype.

**Explicitly still OUT:** a multi-framework **authoring/import engine** — the
ability to define a *new* framework from scratch or import another org's. Editing
the fields of the one framework you own is administration; a framework *builder*
is the platform, and it stays deferred until a pilot earns it. Hold this line so
"tune my framework" does not quietly grow into "build the authoring platform."

**Taxonomy mapping.** ICB4 nests under three competence *areas*
(Perspective / People / Practice), so it maps cleanly onto the generic
domain → competency → indicator shape used by the long-term platform:
competence **area** → domain, competence **element** (28) → competency,
**KCI** (132) → indicator. The prototype ships this one mapping only.

**Prototype scope boundary.** The detailed data model, the self/manager/agreed
state machine, the rollup computation, and the access-control matrix are
deliberately left to `/plan-eng-review` (the next step). This doc fixes the
wedge and the constraints; it does not attempt to be the engineering spec. The
items flagged below (rollup port, auth, access control) are named so they are
not silently dropped in that handoff, not designed here.

## Constraints

- Prototype must be **fast to build** and **usable by real PMs this cycle** — not
  a platform buildout.
- Framework content is fixed: IPMA ICB4 v4.0.1, APM 0–5 Application scale,
  targets from the selected benchmark profile (default "Intermediate").
- Solo founder building with AI assistance; scope must fit that.
- **Access control is a real requirement, not a convention.** The workbook's
  assessor-only content — the target level, priority, the "reason" rationale,
  the KIB guidance notes, and the APM mapping — is deleted before the file is
  issued to the PM. The app must enforce the equivalent: the PM's view excludes
  that content (and self-scores blind to targets) until the assessment reaches
  the agreed/locked state. This is an access-control requirement the eng-review
  must design, not an open question.
- **Purpose fixes the tone: it supports a decision, never gates one.** The
  framework exists to (1) build each PM's training plan and (2) inform project
  assignment. Judgement stays human. The app must never render a pass/fail or
  approve/reject verdict — even the inherited control-level target must not read
  as a pass line (brief §10.2, still open).
- **Rating scale — decided.** APM 0–5 for the prototype, built as a **swappable
  module** so a PDCF variant can be added after cycle 1 and compared on real
  scoring data (per brief §6). Two confirmed refinements:
  - **Plain-language glosses.** Each level shows a plain, second-person helper
    text ("I need guidance…" / "I do this on my own…") beside the label; APM's
    canonical wording is preserved and reachable. The gloss must stay a *faithful*
    restatement of the APM level (drift silently changes the scale). This captures
    PDCF's self-assessment ease without switching scales.
  - **Present level 0 as a separate "not applicable / no awareness" escape**, so a
    PM effectively chooses among 5 substantive rungs (Aware→Expert) — "feels like
    5" with zero target-layer change.
  - Never hardwire APM; the rollup computes on stored 0–5 numbers, PM picks a
    label, never a number.
  - **Cost of switching the primary scale to PDCF (recorded, so it isn't
    re-litigated):** the *code* cost is low (swappable module). The *content* cost
    is high and unshortcuttable — PDCF has no published per-competence targets for
    ICB4, so all 132 targets lose their external anchor and become judgement
    calls; PDCF's 5 levels collapse APM's Practised/Competent (the
    supervised-vs-independent boundary where most KIB PMs sit), so every target is
    re-judged across that seam; the 14 APM benchmark profiles don't map to PDCF
    grades; thresholds re-tune. It costs weeks and the "targets are externally
    published, not opinion" defensibility. Verdict: don't switch the scale to get
    "5 not 6" — the glosses + level-0-as-escape already deliver that. Keep PDCF as
    a post-cycle-1 parallel variant, decided on real score distributions.
- **Sharia-compliant bank: language constraint.** Never use "interest" in the
  financial sense anywhere in the UI or copy; use "profit rate", "rate of
  return", "return". Define technical terms on first use (plain-language house
  style).

## Premises

1. **The pain is in the assessment loop, not the authoring engine.** Every stated
   pain is about collecting, reconciling, rolling up, and trending assessments.
   None is about defining or importing frameworks. Building the generic
   import/define engine first is premature generalization — it is the seductive
   part to architect and the part nobody is feeling pain about.
2. **A sponsor mandate for the outcome is not a mandate for the platform.** The
   Excel already satisfies the mandate. Software must earn its existence on
   completion and rollup, or it is architecture in search of a problem.
3. **The right to sell a multi-framework platform is earned by delighting one
   organization with one framework first.** Generality is the last thing to add,
   not the first.
4. **Completion-rate improvement is the central product bet and is currently
   unobserved.** If PMs would finish the Excel just as readily, the strongest
   consumer-facing reason for the web app weakens.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Single framework, hardcoded (CHOSEN)
ICB4 baked into the app. No authoring UI. All effort on the assessment loop and
the PMO rollup. Fastest path to a real assessment with real PMs. Proves the two
software-only values (completion, live rollup). Risk: a future second framework
means real refactoring.

### Approach B: Generic import/define engine first
Build the framework schema (domain → competency → indicator → scoring) and
authoring/import UI from day one; load ICB4 as the first example. Bets on the
platform thesis before any organization is delighted. Slower to first real
assessment; most of the initial build serves a need no one has voiced yet.

### Approach C: Config-driven data model, one framework, no authoring UI
Store the framework as data (not hardcoded constants) so the assessment loop
reads it generically, but ship no import/authoring UI and only ever load ICB4.
Near-zero extra cost over A, and it removes most of A's future-refactor risk
without buying any of B's premature platform surface.

## Recommended Approach

Approach C, essentially — Approach A's discipline with C's config-driven storage,
which the editable content and the swappable scale now *require* rather than merely
suggest: **model the framework as seeded data, not hardcoded constants.** Ship a
**single-framework admin editor** over the tunable layer (target, active/inactive,
priority, reason, and the KIB additional-info field) — but **no multi-framework
authoring or import engine** and no framework *builder*. User-facing scope stays
narrow: one framework (ICB4), the assessment loop, the admin editor for that one
framework, and the PMO rollup. The line to hold: nothing that exists only to serve
"any framework, any org."

This is the **one deliberate, cost-justified exception** to Premise 1's warning
against premature generalization: config-driven storage plus single-framework
admin editing builds no multi-framework platform surface, so it buys the tuning
the founder actually needs (the framework and scale are still moving) without the
trap the premise warns about. No import engine, no framework builder, no second
framework — until a pilot earns them.

**Reproduce, don't reinvent, the rollup.** The workbook's rollup — the weighted
per-CE score, the Role-Ready / Minor-Gap / Capability-Deficit thresholds, the
inactive-control handling, and the snapshot/versioning rules — is the real
engineering in this prototype, not incidental CRUD. The Excel `Results`, `Scale`,
and `Framework` sheets are the authoritative spec to port; treat "port the rollup
faithfully" as a distinct workstream in eng-review.

## Results & Reporting Display

Carried from the handover brief §8 (settled) so the doc is complete. Reported by
**ICB4 competence element** (28 rows) — PMs are assessed by CE and reported by CE,
no translation between what they answered and what they see. APM is the source of
target *values*, not of the reporting structure.

Per-person results layout:
1. **Header** — name, date, benchmark profile applied, cycle.
2. **Area summary** — three horizontal bars: Perspective / People / Practice.
3. **Competence detail** — bar-on-bar, **actual vs target**, on the 0–5 scale.
4. **Gap list** — sorted by gap size: CE, target, actual, weakest control, 3-tier
   status (Role Ready / Minor Gap / Capability Deficit).
5. **Narrative lines** — formula-driven, one above each chart.

Display rules (from §7 / §8):
- **No radar chart** — 28 axes is unreadable; a sorted bar list answers "where are
  the gaps" better.
- **Numbers, not percentages, on results charts** (0–5 scale). Converting a
  6-point label scale to a percentage implies equal intervals that don't exist.
  Labels are used in the scoring interface; numbers appear on the results charts.
- **Actual = mean of Agreed scores across the CE's active controls**, with the
  **weakest control shown alongside** so a single serious gap isn't absorbed by
  the average. Inactive controls contribute nothing, regardless of any score.
- **Targets are not rolled up** — they are APM's published values. Where a CE
  spans two APM competences with different values, the row shows the dominant
  value.
- **Provenance is not shown in results** — the report shows target vs actual;
  where a target came from (APM-published vs KIB-derived) lives on the Controls
  data only.

**Reference grammar:** the Provek/APM PMA individual report — bar-on-bar with
benchmark overlay, a narrative line above each chart, and a
recommended-development table turning findings into action. Take the bar-on-bar,
the narrative lines, and the development table; leave the rest (three of its four
dimensions are *tested* instruments, not self-report, so they have no data here).

## Open Questions

1. **What decision does the PMO rollup drive?** Training budget, promotion,
   staffing on complex projects? The rollup's design should serve that decision.
   Confirm it with the Head of PMO before building the dashboard.
2. **Does the online form actually raise completion?** (See The Assignment.)
3. **Disagreement path.** The self → manager → agreed flow assumes the 1-on-1
   always lands on an agreed score. What happens when it doesn't — manager
   override, escalation to the Head of PMO, or leave-as-gap? Decide the tie-break
   before build.
4. **Auth — position taken, not just flagged.** SSO/AD integration is a classic
   solo-founder time sink and the likeliest thing to blow the "usable this cycle"
   goal. **Recommendation: ship the pilot on a simple allowlist of individually
   whitelisted email addresses (you add specific people — not limited to KIB
   domains), and defer SSO/AD until after the pilot proves value.** Overturn this
   only if KIB security policy forbids a non-SSO app touching this data — confirm
   that with the Head of PMO. Note that auth and the access-control requirement
   together (not either alone) are the pairing most likely to break the timeline,
   since "blind until agreed" must be enforced at the API layer, not just the UI.

(Note: access control and config-driven storage were open questions in an earlier
draft; both are now settled above — access control as a Constraint requirement,
storage as config-driven seed data under Recommended Approach.)

## Success Criteria

- One full assessment cycle completed **online** by the KIB PM pilot cohort:
  self-score → manager score → agreed score, for every active control.
- **Completion:** measurably higher share of invited PMs finish without chasing,
  versus the Excel baseline (record the baseline first).
- **PMO rollup:** the Head of PMO sees the live 28-CE capability profile
  (Role-Ready / Minor-Gap / Capability-Deficit) with no manual file merging.
- **Time-to-insight:** from "assessment window closes" to "PMO sees results"
  drops from days of chasing-and-merging to near-real-time.

## Distribution Plan

Internal web service for the pilot (single tenant, KIB PMO). Deployment pipeline
to be defined at build time — likely a single hosted app, access controlled by an
allowlist of individually whitelisted email addresses. Multi-tenant SaaS and
public subscription are out of scope for the prototype, gated behind a delighted
pilot.

## Dependencies

- The ICB4 framework content (the "Framework" sheet) as the seed dataset:
  28 competence elements and **133 KCIs total — 132 active, 1 inactive** (the
  seed must include the inactive control so the inactive-handling logic has data
  to operate on; inactive controls contribute nothing to any rollup or gap).
  Plus the APM 0–5 scale, per-CE targets, the Measures reference set, and the

- Confirmation from the Head of PMO on what the rollup is used to decide.
- A named pilot cohort of PMs and their managers.

## The Assignment

Don't run a throwaway Excel exercise with your PMs — that double-works them and
wastes their time. The online cycle *is* the test. Two low-cost steps instead:

1. **Instrument the prototype to measure completion for real.** Build the app so
   cycle 1 captures, per PM: whether they finished, and median time-to-complete.
   No parallel Excel run needed — the numbers come from the tool itself.
2. **Set a cheap baseline from what you already know** (5 minutes at your desk,
   zero PM time): has any prior assessment or form-filling exercise with this team
   had completion or lateness problems? If yes, completion is a real wedge worth
   building for. If people generally finish what you send them, your wedge is the
   PMO rollup and the structured self/manager/agreed flow, not completion — and
   that tells you where to put effort first.

The point isn't the spreadsheet; it's knowing *before you over-invest* whether
completion is the problem the software is solving here, or whether the rollup is.
