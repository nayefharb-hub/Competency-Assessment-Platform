# Competency Assessment — user guide

For KIB's PMO. Two audiences: **project managers**, who assess themselves, and
the **Head of PMO**, who reviews, revises and approves.

This is a manual, not a history. It describes the product as it is today. What
changed and why lives in `docs/pilot-feedback.md` and the release notes.

---

## What this is

You score yourself against the **IPMA ICB4** competence framework — 3 areas, 28
competence elements, 132 statements ("controls") you answer one at a time. The
Head of PMO then reviews your answers and can adjust any of them. The result is
a picture of capability against the **APM role-profile targets** for a project
manager: where you are ready, where there is a gap, and which single control is
weakest in each competency.

**It supports a decision. It does not make one.** There is no pass, no fail, and
no overall verdict.

---

## Before you start

Your account is created for you by the Head of PMO — you cannot sign yourself
up. You will be given a **work email** and a **starting password**.

1. Go to the app and sign in with that email and password.
2. You will be asked to **change your password** on first sign-in. Do it; you
   cannot reach the rest of the app until you have.
3. You land on the home screen. The menu at the top has **Self-assessment** and
   **Results**. The Head of PMO also sees **Review**, **Analysis**, **People**
   and **Framework**.

If you see *"you have not been assigned an assessment"*, the cycle has not been
assigned to you yet. Ask the Head of PMO — it takes them one click.

# Part 1 — For project managers

## Finding your work

**Self-assessment** in the menu takes you to *Your self-assessment* — the hub.
It shows the three areas of the framework, how many controls you have scored in
each, and roughly how long the rest will take. Each area opens to its
competencies.

You do not have to work in order. You can jump to any control at any time.

## Scoring one control

Each control screen has two halves:

- **Left** — what the control asks. The competency leads at the top (for example
  *4.3.1 Strategy*, with "3 of 5 in this competency" beside it), then the ICB4
  statement itself, then **Measures** — examples of what doing this looks like.
  Measures are *reference only and are never scored*.
- **Right** — your answer. Six levels, and an optional evidence box.

### The six levels

| | Level | Means |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Unaware | You have not encountered this |
| 1 | Aware | You know of it but have not applied it |
| 2 | Practised | You have done it, with support |
| 3 | Competent | You do it reliably on your own |
| 4 | Proficient | You do it well and others rely on you for it |
| 5 | Expert | You set the standard and develop others in it |

You choose a **label**, never a number. The numbers exist underneath for the
maths.

**Your target is deliberately hidden while you score.** The screen says so:
*"🔒 target hidden while you self-score"*. Seeing the target first anchors the
answer, which is exactly what a self-assessment must not do. You will see
targets on your results, after submission.

### Evidence

*Evidence or example (optional, not scored)* — one line describing when you did
this. It is not marked and does not change your score. It is the most useful
thing you can give the Head of PMO when they review, and it is what you will
thank yourself for at the next cycle.

### Keyboard

You can answer without touching the mouse:

- **0–5** picks that level.
- **Enter** confirms and moves to the next control.

Typing in the evidence box is safe — numbers typed there go into the text, not
into your score.

## What "saved" means

**Picking a level saves nothing.** Change your mind as often as you like.

**The button is the save.** *"Next control →"* confirms your answer and moves
on. While an answer is unconfirmed you will see *"Not saved yet — Next control
saves it"* under the panel.

You never wait for the save. The answer is handed to a queue and the screen
moves immediately, so a slow connection never slows you down.

**Skipping is allowed.** With no level picked, the button reads *"Skip for now
→"*. The hole stays visible in your progress count and you cannot submit until
it is filled.

**Everything else does not save.** Previous, the menu, signing out — none of
them commit an answer you did not confirm. A pick you did not confirm is a pick
you were still thinking about.

## When the connection drops

The app keeps working. Confirmed answers are stored on your device and sent when
you are back online — the hint changes to *"Saved on this device — it will send
when you are back online."*

A banner appears anywhere in the app while answers are waiting, with a count and
a **Retry now** button. It disappears by itself once everything has landed.
Answers are never silently discarded, and they survive closing the tab.

While you are offline the app will not navigate — links and Continue are
disabled rather than taking you to a browser error page. Answering carries on
normally.

## Finishing a competency

When you confirm the last control of a competency, the panel is replaced — in
place, without a page change — by a short card:

- **"<Competency> complete"** and how many controls that was.
- A **recap** of every control in it with the level you gave. **Any row is
  clickable** to go back and change that answer. This is the easiest moment to
  revise something.
- **Next:** the competency you are about to start.
- **Continue →** carries straight on to the next competency's first unanswered
  control. **Enter** does the same.
- **Take a break** returns you to the list.

If you skipped something in that competency, no card appears — the competency is
not finished, and the button will have said *"Next control"* rather than *"Finish
this competency"*.

## Picking up where you left off

The app remembers your position on each device. **Self-assessment** in the menu
brings you back to the hub, and the hub offers *"Pick up where you left off"*.

Your answers live on the server, so any device shows the same progress.

## Reviewing and submitting

**Your assessment** (the controls list) shows every control, its status, and
your progress out of 132. You can filter to what is still unscored and jump
straight to any control.

**Submit for review** becomes available only when **all 132 active controls have
an answer**. If something is missing the button stays disabled — and if you try
anyway, the server refuses. This is not a formality: an incomplete assessment
cannot be reviewed fairly.

After you submit:

- Your assessment is locked. Scores can no longer be changed.
- A banner tells you the date you submitted and that it is with the Head of PMO.
- You can still read everything.

## Reading your results

**Results** shows, per competence element:

- **Your level** — the mean of the assessor's scores across the active controls
  in that element.
- **The target** — the published APM benchmark for the profile you are assessed
  against (Intermediate by default). Targets are published values; they are
  never averaged or derived.
- **The weakest control** — the single lowest-scored control in that element,
  named. An element can sit at target overall and still contain one control that
  needs work; this is where you see it.
- **Health**:

| Band | Means |
|---|---|
| **Role Ready** | At or above target |
| **Minor Gap** | Within half a level below target |
| **Capability Deficit** | More than half a level below target, or any single control two or more levels below its own target |

The levels shown are the **assessor's**, which is the authoritative record. Your
own self-scores are kept, and the Head of PMO can see both side by side.

# Part 2 — For the Head of PMO

## Setting up people

**People** manages who can use the app.

- **Add person** — full name, job title, work email, and a role:
  - *Project manager* — takes the assessment.
  - *Assessor* — reviews and approves.
  - *Administrator* — everything.
- Adding someone puts them on the **allowlist** and generates a **starting
  password**, shown once. Give it to them; they must change it on first sign-in.
- **Reset password** issues a new starting password.
- **Archive, don't delete.** Archiving removes someone's access while keeping
  their assessment history intact.

## Assigning the cycle

On the same screen, tick the people you want and **Assign selected**. That
creates their assessment for the current cycle in *draft*. Nobody can start
until this happens, and each person can only hold one assessment per cycle.

An assignment nobody has touched can be **withdrawn**. Once a single answer
exists, withdrawal is refused — the work is theirs, not yours to erase.

## Watching progress

**People** shows who has started and how far they are. **Analysis** shows *how*
each assessment was filled in rather than what it said: median time per control,
how long an assessment took, and which ones have stalled. A project manager can
see this for their own assessment and only their own.

## Reviewing and revising

**Review** lists submitted assessments. Opening one gives you *Review & revise*
for that person:

- Every control, with **their self-score pre-filled**.
- You can accept a score or override it. Your score is the authoritative one.
- **Accept all remaining** takes the self-scores you have not touched, so you
  only have to act where you disagree.
- Their evidence sits beside each control.

Your revisions are what feed the results and the health bands.

## Approving

**Approve assessment** closes the cycle for that person. On approval the
**targets are frozen** against the record, so the results stay meaningful even
if the benchmark profile is edited later.

The state moves *draft → submitted → approved*. Approving an incomplete review
is refused by the server, not just hidden in the UI.

## What the tool will not do

- **It never edits ICB4 source text.** The framework wording is the standard's.
  KIB's own clarifications are added in a separate field alongside it, never on
  top of it.
- **It never shows a PM their target while they self-score.**
- **It never rolls up or averages a target.** Targets are published values.
- **It never issues a pass, a fail, or a ranking.** It reports capability
  against a profile and shows where the gaps are. What to do about them is a
  conversation, not an output.

## Troubleshooting

**"You have not been assigned an assessment."**
The cycle has not been assigned to you. Ask the Head of PMO.

**A banner says answers are waiting to send.**
Your connection dropped. The answers are safe on your device. Leave the tab open
and they will send themselves; **Retry now** forces an attempt. Do not re-answer
those controls.

**The Submit button is disabled.**
Something is unanswered. Open *Your assessment*, filter to what is not scored,
and fill the gaps.

**I answered a control but the count looks one behind.**
Progress counts are read from the server, which can lag your most recent answer
by a moment. It catches up on the next screen. The competency card and the
control list always reflect what you have actually answered.

**I finished a competency but got no card.**
One of its controls was skipped. The button says *"Next control"* rather than
*"Finish this competency"* when that happens.

**I forgot my password.**
Ask the Head of PMO for a reset; they can issue a new starting password.

**I submitted too early.**
Ask the Head of PMO. A submitted assessment is locked to you but they can act on
it.

## Glossary

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| **Area** | One of the three top-level groups in ICB4 (People, Practice, Perspective) |
| **Competence element / competency** | One of 28 named capabilities, e.g. *4.3.1 Strategy*. The unit results roll up to |
| **Control** | One statement you answer. 132 of them |
| **Cycle** | One round of assessment, one per person per year |
| **Self-score** | Your answer |
| **Assessor score** | The Head of PMO's answer. The authoritative one |
| **Target** | The published APM benchmark level for your profile |
| **Profile** | The APM role profile assessed against. Intermediate by default |
| **Weakest control** | The lowest-scored control inside a competency, shown beside its average |
